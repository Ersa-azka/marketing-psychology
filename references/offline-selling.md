# Offline / Direct Selling Psychology

For face-to-face, phone, and WhatsApp/DM selling. Broadcast copy talks AT a
crowd; direct selling is a live loop with one human who can push back. The
psychology shifts from "grab attention" to "build trust and lower resistance in
real time."

Golden rule: **the person who asks better questions controls the conversation —
not the one who talks more.** Amateurs pitch. Pros diagnose.

## The arc

1. Open & build rapport
2. Diagnose (discovery)
3. Pitch to the diagnosis
4. Handle objections
5. Close
6. Follow up

---

## 1. Open & build rapport

People buy from those they like and trust, and both are decided fast. Build it:

- **Match & mirror** — subtly match their pace, energy, volume, and vocabulary.
  A slow, careful buyer distrusts a fast talker. In text: match their message
  length, formality, emoji use.
- **Lower the guard** — don't open in "selling mode." Be a person first. A
  genuine, specific observation or question beats a scripted greeting.
- **Earn the right to pitch** — nobody wants to be sold to on contact. Warm the
  interaction before you present anything.

WhatsApp/DM note: don't dump a paragraph pitch on first contact. One short,
relevant, curiosity-or-value message. Get a reply (micro-commitment) first.

---

## 2. Diagnose — discovery before pitch

Let them describe their problem in their own words; you'll sell it back to them
in those exact words later. Question types (SPIN-style):

- **Situation** — understand their context (a few, not an interrogation).
- **Problem** — surface the pain: "bagian mana yang paling ngeselin selama ini?"
- **Implication** — make the cost real: "kalau dibiarin, efeknya ke apa?" This is
  where desire is built — they talk themselves into needing a fix.
- **Need-payoff** — let them state the value: "kalau ini beres, seberapa
  ngaruh?"

Talk ~30%, listen ~70%. Take what they give you and feed it back — people trust
their own words more than yours.

---

## 3. Pitch to the diagnosis

Now, and only now, present — tied directly to what they told you:

- Lead with the **emotional job** you uncovered, prove with logic.
- **One benefit at a time**, each mapped to a pain they named.
- Use **their words**: "tadi kamu bilang capek ngejar-ngejar supplier — nah ini
  yang beresin itu."
- Show, don't just claim (mechanism, sample, a similar customer's result).
- Check in ("masuk gak sejauh ini?") to keep it a dialogue, not a monologue.

---

## 4. Handle objections — agree first, never argue

An objection is usually not a "no" — it's "I'm not reassured yet." Winning the
argument loses the sale. Instead:

- **Feel–Felt–Found**: "Paham kok kenapa mikir gitu (feel). Banyak klien awalnya
  mikir sama (felt). Ternyata yang mereka temuin… (found)."
- **Reframe** the objection into a reason to buy: "justru karena budget ketat,
  makanya jangan sampai salah beli dua kali."
- **Isolate**: "selain harga, ada hal lain yang bikin ragu?" — surfaces the real
  objection instead of chasing decoys.

Common objections & the psychology:

- **"Mahal."** Usually = "belum lihat nilainya." Re-anchor against the cost of
  the problem or the cheaper-but-fails alternative. Never drop price
  immediately; that confirms it was overpriced and trains them to push.
- **"Pikir-pikir dulu."** Usually = an unspoken doubt. Gently isolate it: "boleh,
  biasanya kalau 'pikir dulu' itu soal harga atau soal yakin-nggaknya hasil?"
- **"Nanti aja."** Present bias / no urgency. Make the cost of waiting real (if
  it's true), or lower the entry step so starting is easy.

---

## 5. Close — calm assumption, then silence

- **Assumptive close**: proceed as if it's decided — "enaknya dikirim Selasa apa
  Kamis?" (choice of *how*, not *whether*).
- **Summary close**: recap the benefits they agreed to, then ask.
- **Trial close** earlier to test temperature: "kalau cocok, mau langsung atau
  ada yang mau ditanya lagi?"
- **After you ask for the sale, go quiet.** The silence is uncomfortable — let
  them fill it. Talking through the silence talks yourself out of the sale.
- Make saying yes physically/mechanically easy (link ready, form pre-filled,
  next step obvious).

---

## 6. Price & negotiation

- **Anchor high first**, then land — the real number feels like a win.
- **Never split the difference reflexively**; trade concessions for something
  ("bisa turun kalau ambil dua").
- **Walk-away power** is the strongest position — someone willing to lose the
  deal negotiates from strength. Don't fake it; cultivate real alternatives.
- Frame price **per unit of value or time** ("15rb/hari") when that helps, or
  against the problem's cost — never naked against zero.
- Silence after stating price. Don't rush to justify or discount.

---

## 7. Follow up (where most sales are actually made)

Most sales happen after the first contact, yet most sellers quit after one try.
Follow up with **new value each time**, not "just checking in" — a relevant tip,
a case, an answer to the doubt you sensed. Persistent-but-useful beats
persistent-and-annoying. Track where each person stalled and address that exact
thing next.
