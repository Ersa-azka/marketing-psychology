# Persuasion Principles — Applied

The point of this file is application, not theory. For each lever: what it is,
when it wins, how to execute it without sounding like a textbook, and how it
fails (so you can avoid the amateur version).

## Table of contents
1. The seven levers (Cialdini, applied)
2. Cognitive biases that move buyers
3. Awareness stages (Schwartz) — the most important diagnostic
4. Market sophistication — why the same claim stops working
5. Emotional drivers — what people actually buy

## 1. The seven levers, applied

**Reciprocity.** Give something real and unexpected first; the ask feels fair
afterward. In content: a genuinely useful tip, a free diagnosis, a sample. The
amateur version is a "free ebook" nobody wanted — that reads as bait. The give
must have real standalone value even if they never buy.

**Commitment & consistency.** People stay consistent with small choices they've
already made. Get a micro-yes before the macro-yes: a quiz answer, a reply, a
"which one's more you?", a free trial they set up themselves. Once someone has
publicly or actively taken a small step, the big step feels like just being
consistent with who they already are.

**Social proof.** Three multipliers make it work: **specific** (a number, a
name, a before/after), **credible** (real, checkable), and **similar** (the
proof-person looks like the reader). "Dipakai 3 kafe di Seminyak, salah satunya
[nama]" destroys "dipercaya banyak pelanggan". Weakest form: unnamed mass
claims. Strongest: a named person the reader could imagine being.

**Authority.** Show, don't claim. Demonstrate the mechanism ("kenapa kayu kami
gak melengkung: dikeringkan oven sampai kadar air 12%"), cite specific
experience, or borrow it (named clients, certifications, media). Explaining the
*why* behind how something works is itself an authority signal — experts explain
mechanisms; pretenders assert results.

**Liking.** We buy from people we like: similarity, genuine compliments,
familiarity, and shared identity. In copy this is voice — talk like one of them,
about what they care about, with the in-group's language.

**Scarcity & loss aversion.** Loss looms larger than gain (~2x). So frame the
**cost of not acting**, not just the benefit of acting. Real limits only: true
stock caps, real deadlines, genuine capacity ("cuma ambil 4 proyek custom per
bulan"). Fake scarcity is the fastest way to burn trust permanently — one caught
lie poisons every future claim.

**Unity.** Deeper than liking — a shared identity ("kita yang jualan online",
"sesama pejuang UMKM", "orang yang ngerti kopi beneran"). Or a shared enemy (the
overpriced middleman, the plastic knockoff, the ribet-nya birokrasi). A named
enemy bonds an audience faster than a stated benefit.

## 2. Cognitive biases that move buyers

- **Anchoring** — the first number sets the frame. Show the "coret" price, the
  competitor's price, or the cost-of-the-problem before your price.
- **Framing** — "90% lemak-free" beats "10% lemak"; "hemat 2 juta setahun" beats
  "50rb/bulan". Same fact, different felt value.
- **Decoy effect** — a third, deliberately worse option makes your target option
  look obviously right (small / **medium** / large pricing).
- **Endowment & IKEA effect** — people overvalue what feels theirs or what they
  helped build. Free trials, "desain bareng", "kavling nama kamu" create
  ownership before purchase.
- **Zero-risk / guarantee** — removing the last shred of risk converts more than
  a discount. A strong guarantee often beats a lower price.
- **Present bias** — people over-value now. An instant small bonus can beat a
  bigger delayed one.
- **Bandwagon** — "yang lain udah pindah ke…" leverages fear of being left out.
- **Contrast** — sell the expensive thing right after showing the pain/cost, not
  in a vacuum.

Use one or two per piece. Stacking them all reads as a scam.

## 3. Awareness stages (Schwartz) — diagnose this first

Where is the reader's head *right now*? The entire angle changes:

| Stage | They know… | So you lead with… |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing; don't know they have a problem | a story/pattern-break that names a hidden problem |
| Problem-aware | they hurt, not that solutions exist | agitate the pain, then reveal a path exists |
| Solution-aware | solutions exist, not yours | why your *type* of solution wins |
| Product-aware | your product, not convinced | proof, differentiation, risk-removal, offer |
| Most-aware | everything; just need a nudge | the deal, the deadline, the CTA — short |

The classic generic-content mistake is pitching product features to a
problem-aware audience that doesn't yet care who you are. Meet them where they
are.

## 4. Market sophistication — why the same claim stops working

The more times a market has heard a claim, the less it lands. Five levels:

1. **First to market** — state the plain claim. "Gula kelapa organik."
2. **Claims are common** — make a bigger/sharper claim. "Gula kelapa indeks
   glikemik terendah."
3. **Claims exhausted** — introduce a **mechanism** (the how/why it works).
   "…karena dipanen dari nira bunga, bukan buah."
4. **Mechanisms exhausted** — make the mechanism bigger/more elaborate, or prove
   it harder.
5. **Everything exhausted** — sell **identity**. Stop competing on claims; become
   the brand for a certain kind of person. Sell who they become.

Diagnosis: how crowded/skeptical is this market? A saturated market needs
mechanism or identity, not louder adjectives.

## 5. Emotional drivers — what people actually buy

Nobody buys a drill; they buy a hole — and really they buy the shelf, and really
the feeling of a home that's got it together. Trace every product down to the
emotional job:

- **Status** — to be seen as successful, tasteful, ahead.
- **Security** — to stop worrying (money, health, safety, the future).
- **Belonging** — to be part of a group, accepted, not left out.
- **Freedom / control** — time, autonomy, escape.
- **Identity** — to become or confirm who they want to be.
- **Relief** — to make a specific, present pain stop.
- **Love / care** — to provide for or impress someone they love.

Method: ask "and that gets them what?" repeatedly until you hit an emotion.
Write to the emotion; supply logic (specs, guarantees, ROI) as the armor they'll
use to justify the emotional decision to themselves and others.
